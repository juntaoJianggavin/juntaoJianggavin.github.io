Highlights

Radev is a decision-native multimodal model for 3D abdominal CT interpretation. It reads a whole CT volume together with a clinical question and a small candidate set, and emits a calibrated probability distribution over the candidates in a single forward pass, without autoregressive decoding or free-text generation. Anatomical grounding tells the model where to look, and counterfactual-calibrated training tells it how sure to be. We evaluate Radev on CT-Verdict, a benchmark of single-token verdicts (binary finding presence, multiple choice, and ordinal severity) with an IID cohort and a prediction-only OOD cohort.

(1) Decision-native readout. A restricted softmax over single-token candidates yields the full answer distribution (probabilities, not just an argmax) in one forward pass.

(2) Anatomical grounding. A frozen 3D encoder pools per-organ features into an evidence ledger, and each question binds to its target organs and their high-resolution 2D evidence slices.

(3) Counterfactual confidence calibration. Counterfactual-calibrated training (CCT) ablates the bound evidence and penalizes overconfidence when the answer should not change.

(4) CT-Verdict benchmark. 223,286 training questions and 56,825 IID test questions (binary findings, multiple choice, ordinal severity), plus 2,807 OOD questions on external volumes (prediction-only).

Framework

Framework of the proposed Radev. (a) The Organ-Grounded Visual Encoder uses organ masks and learned mask queries to extract 3D organ tokens. Question-selected organ tokens attend to 2D slice features within their anatomical bounds. The language backbone and candidate scoring head predict an answer distribution in a single forward pass. (b) Training proceeds through projector–evidence alignment, verdict fine-tuning, and Counterfactual-Calibrated Training (CCT). (c) CCT constructs factual, target-ablated, control-ablated, and evidence-blind views by intervening on visual features. The first three views receive task supervision, while the evidence-blind view receives a confidence-margin penalty.

CT-Verdict Benchmark

Overview of CT-Verdict. (a) Benchmark construction process: radiology reports are parsed into structured findings; synthesis rules over presence, attributes, and relations turn each finding into candidate questions; questions are generated deterministically and their labels verified by three LLM readers with human spot-checking; finally, a study-level split separates training from test. (b) Examples of the three question types: binary presence (noul), multi-way choice, and ordinal severity (score). The release contains 223,286 training questions and 56,825 IID test questions (280,111 total), plus 2,807 OOD questions on external volumes.

Confidence Calibration

Reliability resolution analysis. Each point represents a model plotted by its reliability error (REL) and resolution (RES), computed from the binned Murphy decomposition of the top-label Brier score. The dashed line marks the RES = REL boundary.

Accuracy under confidence-based rejection. Samples are rejected in ascending order of predictive confidence. The rejection rate is the percentage of all samples rejected, and accuracy is computed over the retained samples.
